# US DOT — Airline Delay, Cancellation, and Refund Rules

Rules enforced by the U.S. Department of Transportation for flights to, from,
or within the United States.

## Refunds for cancelled or significantly changed flights

Passengers are entitled to a full refund to the original form of payment when
the airline cancels a flight or makes a significant change and the passenger
chooses not to accept the alternative offered. This applies regardless of the
reason for the cancellation, including weather.

A "significant change" includes:
- A departure or arrival time change of 3 hours or more for domestic flights,
  or 6 hours or more for international flights.
- A change to the departure or arrival airport.
- An increase in the number of connections.

Refunds must be issued within 7 business days for credit card purchases and
20 calendar days for cash or check purchases.

## Delays

There is no federal requirement for airlines to compensate passengers for
delays. Compensation, meal vouchers, or hotel accommodation for delays are
governed by each airline's own customer service commitments, not by DOT rule.

## Tarmac delays

For domestic flights, airlines may not keep passengers on the tarmac for more
than 3 hours (4 hours for international) without allowing them to deplane,
except for safety, security, or air traffic control reasons.

## Baggage

Airlines must refund checked baggage fees if the bag is declared lost, and
must compensate passengers for reasonable expenses caused by delayed baggage.
